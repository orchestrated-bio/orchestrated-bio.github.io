Worked example · prexasertib · NCT02203513

Graded against a known answer.

A CHK1 inhibitor worked in about a third of patients. The trial team spent a full translational program finding out why, published the answer, and deposited the data.

We ran the pipeline against the same public record to see where it landed. Their result is the answer key, so you can grade every step of ours against it.

The answer key

What the trial team published.

Giudice et al. ran prexasertib in BRCA-wild-type, platinum-resistant high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma, then took pre-treatment biopsies from 15 patients and asked what separated the six who benefited from the nine who didn't.

Their finding: replication genes were elevated in the patients who did not benefit — POLE (p=0.011), GINS3 (p=0.02), POLA1 (p=0.037). Their model is that this high-fidelity machinery lets a tumor absorb the replication stress a CHK1 inhibitor creates, by servicing the backup origins that fire when the brake comes off. They followed it with cell-line work: POLA1 silencing plus prexasertib inhibited growth 83% and 75% where either alone did far less, and it still worked in prexasertib-resistant lines.

They also state plainly that no gene survives multiple-testing correction at n=15 (FDR q = 0.87–1), and call the biomarker work hypothesis-generating. The finding rests on the pathway-level signal and the functional validation, not on fifteen biopsies.

Giudice et al., Nat Commun 2024 · PMID 38555285 GSE249587

What this run is not. It is not a discovery, and it is not blind. The GEO record for this dataset names POLA1, POLE, GINS3 and MCM7 in its summary and links the paper, so the answer was available to anyone who downloaded it. What a known-answer run can show is whether the pipeline gets there, and by what route. What it cannot show is whether the pipeline would have gotten there first.

The run

Where our pipeline landed.

Starting from the trial identifier and public sources only: the protocol PDF, a public knowledge graph, and the deposited expression data.

Differential expressionPublic omics

Converges
Testing that neighborhood against responder vs non-responder biopsies: replication effectors POLA1, POLE, GINS3 up in non-responders. The rest of the CHK1 neighborhood flat — CHEK1, CHEK2, CDK1, CDK2, CDC25A/B/C, TP53, ATR, RAD51, RB1.

BoundarySame three genes the authors report. Nominal p-values, uncorrected, n=15. This reproduces their result; it does not independently confirm it. The data is theirs.

ConfoundersSignal integrity

Unresolved
The replication genes do not move alone. Ribosome, base excision repair, homologous recombination and spliceosome rise with them, while the responder side is weighted toward stromal and immune programs. Coordinated movement like that is also what a more proliferative tumor, or a purer one, looks like.

BoundaryNeither the published analysis nor ours separates POLA1 from proliferation rate or stromal content. Regressing out a proliferation score and deconvolving purity would test it. The authors' cell-line work argues the axis is real; the expression data alone cannot.

Escape routesInverse query

Weaker than theirs
Querying the graph backwards from POLA1 returns approved nucleoside analogs: nelarabine, fludarabine, cladribine, clofarabine. The authors went to ST1926, a selective POLA1 inhibitor, and validated it.

BoundaryOur list is looser than the one a specialist reached. These are promiscuous antimetabolites that hit ribonucleotide reductase and several polymerases; the POLA1 annotation is real for clofarabine and thin for nelarabine. A graph edge is a lead to check, not a pharmacology claim.

Orthogonal checkDepMap

Cuts both ways
Across ~1,700 CRISPR-screened lines, TP53 status barely shifts CHEK1 dependency (Cohen's d = 0.17). The synthetic-lethality premise the cohort selection rested on is not a large selective-dependency signal in culture.

BoundaryThis does not refute synthetic lethality in vivo, where replication stress differs from monolayer culture. It does say p53 status alone was never going to be a sufficient selection biomarker.

Reading: the pipeline reached the trial team's own three genes from the public record. It did not discover them, and the confound the authors' cell-line work addresses is one we have not addressed computationally.

Why run it against a solved case

A method is only worth what it does on a question you can check.

On your target, nobody has published the answer. That is the reason to run this, and it is also why you cannot grade the output. You would be taking our word for it.

So we ran it where the answer exists. Giudice's team had the trial, the biopsies, the cell lines, and years. We had the protocol PDF and the public record. The pipeline reached their genes, by a route you can inspect, and missed MCM7 that they report. A method that only shows you its hits is not showing you the method.

The next version of this run is the one worth more: strip the answer out of the inputs, run it blind, and see whether POLA1 still surfaces. Until we publish that, this page shows the route and not the foresight.

Engine

Retrieval agents work the literature, trial registries, chemical and target databases, and public omics in parallel, pulling out claims and cross-checking them. This is the half that scales.

Human

A bioinformatician reviews the output and decides what holds. Every boundary in the table above is a human call: knowing that a flat neighborhood is informative, and that a coordinated replication signature might be proliferation, is judgment rather than retrieval.
