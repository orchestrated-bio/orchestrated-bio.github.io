A worked example: prexasertib.

We ran DrugAdopt on prexasertib, a CHK1 inhibitor in platinum-resistant BRCA-wild-type ovarian cancer, using only public data. The engine produced a biomarker and patient-selection readout spanning the clinical phenotype, mechanism and biomarker biology, pharmacology and exposure, toxicology, and the evidence gaps.

It found a real responder subset, but no public biomarker that yet separates those patients. The mechanism is plausible and the early response rates looked strong; the figures below show both the supporting evidence and where the biomarker case breaks.

Five figures from the report.

Evidence state

What the public evidence supports.

CHK1 is a candidate vulnerability in this population and the biology is coherent, but public evidence does not establish a measurable, load-bearing tumor CHK1-dependence mechanism, and no public biomarker supports patient selection today.

The remaining gaps are explicit: tumor PK paired with a validated pathway-PD readout for the exposure question; a frozen replication-stress score tested in an independent cohort for the biomarker question; and a prospective test of whether biomarker enrichment separates responders. These are the measurements that would strengthen or challenge the patient-selection case, not a go/no-go verdict from DrugAdopt.
