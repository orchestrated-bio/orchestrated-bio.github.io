Worked example · public data only

Nilotinib for Parkinson disease, worked on evidence available before 14 Dec 2020.

Nobody paid for this. We picked an asset whose answer was published later, so you can grade the method against a known result. Each row gives the finding, the source, and the limit of what that source establishes. The third column is the one worth reading.

nilotinib · Parkinson disease evidence frozen 13 Dec 2020 · public sources only
CNS exposurePK/PD

Unresolved
Small studies reported CSF detection, but no matched plasma/CSF pharmacokinetics and no bridge from measured exposure to the concentration c-Abl modulation would require. PMID 30906562

BoundaryDetection in CSF is not exposure at the target site. This says the question was open, not that the drug fails to reach brain.

Target engagementMechanism

Unresolved
No qualified human marker showed the intended CNS pathway moved. Systemic dosing was treated as though it established brain-level engagement. PMID 31841599

BoundaryAbsence of a qualified marker is absence of evidence. The pathway may well move; it had not been shown to.

Mechanism supportPreclinical

Partial
Animal and cell work supported c-Abl involvement in α-synuclein handling. The biology was plausible. PMID 24412932 PMID 23666528

BoundaryPlausible biology in model systems. Carries no information about human target engagement at a tolerable dose.

Safety ceilingTox · SAR

Decision-limiting
The approved label carries QTc, hepatic, vascular, hematologic, and pancreatic liabilities that plausibly cap dose below a CNS-active exposure. FDA NDA 22-068

BoundaryA feasibility constraint from the oncology label. Patient-level risk in a PD population needs clinical review we would refer out.

Potency anchorSAR

Rough screen
Reported CSF concentrations sit far below the ABL1 biochemical activity anchor — roughly 44× on the numbers available. ChEMBL 1862

BoundaryA back-of-envelope screen using total, not unbound, concentrations. Not occupancy, not proof of mechanism failure. Treat as a flag to investigate.

Reading: at the freeze date, the case for another efficacy trial in the same oral formulation rested on an exposure question nobody had answered. That is a gap in the record. It is not a prediction that the trial would fail.

The answer key, kept separate. The randomized NILO-PD trial reported afterward found no efficacy signal and very low CNS exposure (PMID 33315105). We kept it out of the rows above so the reasoning stands on its own. One retrospective case cannot prove a method predicts trial outcomes, and we are not claiming it does. What it shows is that the exposure gap was already legible in the public record.

Engine

Retrieval agents work the literature, trial registries, chemical and target databases, and public omics in parallel, pulling out claims and cross-checking them against each other. This is the half that scales. It reads far more than two people can and flags every place the sources disagree.

Human

Then a bioinformatician goes through the output and decides what survives. Every boundary in the table above is a human call, because knowing that a CSF detection isn't target-site exposure is judgment, not retrieval. The engine assembles the case. A person signs it, and the signature is the part you're paying for.

What gets worked

Eight places an asset tends to break.

Not all eight matter for every drug. We scope to the ones that can change your decision, and we say up front which ones public data can't settle at all.

Mechanism

Target biology, pathway support, expression and dependency evidence across public cohorts.

PK / PD

Exposure, distribution, and whether reported concentrations reach a defensible pharmacology anchor.

Toxicology

Label liabilities, class effects, and whether safety caps the dose below a useful exposure.

SAR

Potency anchors, analogue precedent, and structure-driven arguments for reformulation.

Trial precedent

What has been tried in the indication, how it was designed, how it read out, and why.

Biomarker & stratification

Whether a responder population is definable, and whether the proposed marker survives scrutiny.

RNA-level depth

When the risk is an RNA question (isoform, splicing, modification), we look below the gene-level view where standard analysis stops.

Signal integrity

Whether a reported effect survives batch, composition, and cross-cohort checks, or dissolves under them.

Why this page is built this way

Nobody has to take our word for any of it.

You know your disease better than we will after any engagement. That's not the gap. The gap is that you've been living inside one hypothesis for years, and the evidence that would complicate it is scattered across databases nobody has time to work systematically.

So we don't hand you a verdict to accept on reputation. Every claim above links to its source and states its own limit. Open the sources. If the reasoning holds, that's worth something. If it doesn't, you've lost ten minutes and learned to ignore us, which is more than most diligence lets you find out.

The nilotinib example is unpaid work on a public asset. We picked one whose answer was published later, so the method could be graded against a real result instead of admired in the abstract.

Where we stop. We sign our own analysis: whether a computational or RNA-level signal is real, mechanism and biomarker reasoning, reproducibility of a published claim. Disease-specific clinical judgment, endpoint and regulatory strategy go to a named reviewer. CMC, tox operations, DMPK, formulation, legal and IP we refer out and do not opine on.
