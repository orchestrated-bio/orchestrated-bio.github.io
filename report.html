Research diligence readout

Prexasertib’s target CHK1 is a candidate, mechanistically plausible vulnerability in platinum-resistant BRCA-wild-type HGSOC, but public evidence does not establish a measurable, load-bearing tumor CHK1-dependence mechanism.

Question: Does prexasertib have a biomarker-enrichable, clinically separable development path in platinum-resistant BRCA-wild-type HGSOC?

Primary uncertainty
Exposure-timed tumor drug concentration and validated pathway PD (pCHK1 / pCDC25C) in responders versus non-responders.
Biomarker readiness
Tumor FISH/NGS and cyclin-E1 IHC are feasible but not qualified for selection. Tumor pathway PD and OncoSignature are not publicly qualified.

Systematic evidence review

The public monotherapy phenotype is heterogeneous across three directly on-scope datasets.

The datasets should be reconciled, not collapsed into one “definitive” response rate. The two single-center signals are real, but they do not establish a portable 30% monotherapy expectation. The independent multicenter result materially lowers that expectation without making the earlier observations disappear.

Clinical result

The multicenter result sets the more defensible unselected benchmark.

Comparability note. Disease-control definitions differed across studies. The cohorts 5/6 result used PR + stable disease for at least six months; the multicenter study used stable disease for at least four months.

Biomarker result

No public baseline marker is ready to select patients.

CCNE1 amplification produced a numerical response difference, 19.2% versus 7.4%, but the difference was not significant (p = 0.13). Median PFS was 3.7 months regardless of CCNE1 amplification, and cyclin E1 protein expression did not separate partial responders from patients with stable or progressive disease.

The later NCT02203513 biopsy subset did not rescue the marker. No individual transcript remained differentially expressed after multiplicity correction. DNA-replication pathway enrichment and raw POLA1, POLE and GINS3 associations came from 15 pretreatment RNA-seq samples and remain mechanism-informed resistance hypotheses, not patient-selection markers.

Safety and treatment delivery

Marrow toxicity is manageable, but it is part of the product profile.

Across the 169-patient multicenter study, 72.8% had grade 3 or higher treatment-emergent adverse events and 58.0% had grade 3 or higher treatment-related events. Grade 3–4 neutropenia occurred in 37.9%.
